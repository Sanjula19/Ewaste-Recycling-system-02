Start here: the two power rails

The board has only one 3V3 pin and two GND pins, but five things need power and ground. Everything else in this document assumes these two rails exist, so build them first. 

ESP32  3V3  ──────▶  breadboard RED (+) rail
ESP32  GND  ──────▶  breadboard BLUE (−) rail

then every component takes its power and ground from the rails,
never directly from the board.
Many full-size breadboards split their rails in the middle. If a component on the far half gets no power, bridge the two halves with a short jumper along the same coloured line — or keep everything on one side.

“How do two wires share one 3V3 pin?”

They don't — and they don't need to. One wire leaves the 3V3 pin, and everything else takes power from the rail it feeds. The rails are different from the numbered columns in exactly the way that makes this work:

GroupWhat is joined together
Numbered column (5, 10, 30…)the 5 holes stacked under that one number
Power rail (red / blue strip)the whole long row — 25 to 50 holes
 
ESP32 3V3 ──one jumper──▶ RED (+) rail
                              │
                    ┌─────────┴─────────┐
                    ▼                   ▼
              IR sensor VCC      Colour sensor VIN

Or use the ESP32's own column

If the board is plugged into the breadboard, its 3V3 pin already sits in a numbered column — and the other four holes in that column are connected to it: 

        col 30      ← wherever the ESP32's 3V3 pin lands
   a    ESP32 3V3 pin
   b       o        ← free, and already at 3.3 V
   c       o        ← IR sensor VCC here
   d       o        ← colour sensor VIN here
   e       o        ← still free

Three spare 3.3 V holes, no rail wire needed. Fine for two or three components; switch to the rail once the buzzer and HC-SR04 arrive, since a column only ever gives you four spares. Both are electrically identical — anything sharing a connected group shares the voltage.

Breadboard layout — exact columns

Follow this and everything lands in the right place. The only rule you need: the 5 holes under one number are joined to each other; different numbers are not. So two things in the same column are connected, and two things in different columns are not. 

      col 10   col 11   col 12
  a     o        o        o
  b     o        o        o      the five holes stacked under
  c     o        o        o      ONE number are wired together
  d     o        o        o

Each LED is the same chain, just 15 columns further along:

The mistake that catches everyone: both LED legs in the same column. That shorts the LED out and it can never light. The long and short legs must always be in two different columns — 10 and 14, never 10 and 10.
The three short legs could share one column if you prefer — ground is common, so that is electrically fine and saves two jumpers. The three long legs must stay in separate columns, or the ESP32 loses the ability to drive each lamp independently.

Before you plug USB in

  • Are both LED legs of each lamp in different columns?
  • Does each resistor share a column with its LED's long leg?
  • Is the red rail fed from 3V3 and the blue rail from GND — not crossed?
  • Is anything accidentally bridging the red and blue rails? That is a dead short.
  • Are the sensor's SDA/SCL on RX2/TX2 — not RX0/TX0?
  • Is everything on the same side of the centre channel?

Wired and working today

TCS34725 colour sensor Working

The only sensor in the build. It both detects that an item has arrived and reads its colour.

Sensor pinGoes toSilkscreenPosition
VIN3V3 rail3V3right side, bottom
GNDGND railGNDright side, 2nd from bottom
SDAGPIO 16RX2right side, 10th down
SCLGPIO 17TX2right side, 9th down
3Vo, LED, INTleave unconnected
Power must go to VIN, never 3Vo. 3Vo is the regulator's output. Feeding it back-powers the chip: the white LED lights, so it looks alive, but I²C never responds. Also leave the LED pin unconnected — grounding it switches off the illuminator, and the sensor then reads near-zero the moment an item covers it.

RX2 and TX2 are adjacent, which is why these pins were chosen — no holes to miscount. Settings in firmware: 16× gain, 154 ms integration. Healthy Clear readings land between roughly 1,000 and 20,000.

Status LEDs × 3 Working

Each LED is the same three-part chain. Resistor first, then the LED, then ground.

All three short legs share the ground rail. The three long legs must be in separate columns — put two on the same column and the ESP32 can no longer drive them independently.

Resistor colour code: brown–grey–brown–gold = 180 Ω. Anything from 100 Ω to 1 kΩ works; lower is brighter. Resistors have no polarity, so either way round is fine. LEDs do — backwards they simply won't light, but they aren't damaged.

Echo outputs 5 V into a 3.3 V pin. Connected directly it will damage GPIO 18. It needs a two-resistor divider: 
Echo ──[ 1 kΩ ]──┬──▶ GPIO 18   (this junction ≈ 3.3 V)
                 │
              [ 2 kΩ ]
                 │
                GND

5 V × 2kΩ / (1kΩ + 2kΩ) = 3.33 V

SG90 routing gate + MG996R crusher Blocked

Signal wires go to the ESP32; power must not.

ServoSignal wireSilkscreenPower
SG90 gateGPIO 25D25external 5–6 V
MG996R crusherGPIO 26D26external 5–6 V
 
5–6 V 3 A supply ──┬──▶ SG90    red (V+)
                   ├──▶ MG996R  red (V+)
                   └──[ 1000 µF ]── GND

       supply GND ──┴──▶ ESP32 GND      ← grounds MUST be joined

ESP32 GPIO 25 ──────────▶ SG90    orange (signal)
ESP32 GPIO 26 ──────────▶ MG996R  orange (signal)
Blocked until a 5 V 3 A supply exists. MG996R stalls at ~2.5 A and SG90 at ~700 mA; USB provides 500 mA. Powering them from the board resets the ESP32 every time the crusher moves — and it looks exactly like a firmware crash, so people rewrite working code chasing it. The L298N's 5 V pin is not suitable either: its regulator supplies only 500 mA.

Use the ESP32Servo library, never the stock Servo library — that one is AVR-only and will not compile.

IR obstacle sensor — FC-51 Retry with care

Pin order is not standard

FC-51 boards ship with different header layouts. Read the silkscreen on your module and match by name: 

VCC  GND  OUT     ← one common layout
OUT  GND  VCC     ← another
GND  VCC  OUT     ← another

Match by LABEL, never by position.

Why 3V3 and not 5V

On 5 V the module's OUT also swings to 5 V, and ESP32 GPIOs are not 5 V tolerant — that would damage GPIO 27. Powering from 3V3 keeps OUT at a safe 3.3 V.

Test procedure

  • Wire only the IR sensor — nothing else on the board for this test
  • Plug USB in and touch the module within 5 seconds. It should stay at room temperature; any warmth means unplug at once
  • Upload IRSensorTest and wave your hand — expect OBJECT DETECTED / clear
  • Cool but unresponsive, or hot with correct wiring → the module was damaged and needs replacing

Behaviour and limits

Active LOW — OUT is HIGH when clear, LOW on detection. Rated 2–30 cm, realistically 2–10 cm, and much shorter on dark objects; matte black may not register at all. The small potentiometer on the board adjusts sensitivity.

It is optional for the demo. ItemScan.ino triggers on the colour sensor's own Clear-channel drop, which is what the design PDF specified. To bring the IR sensor back, uncomment the blocks marked [IR] in ItemScan.ino, add pinMode(IR_PIN, INPUT_PULLUP) to setup(), and gate scanItem() on irTriggered(). The pull-up matters: with a plain INPUT, a disconnected OUT wire floats and reads as random detections.

Pins never to use

PinReason
TX0 · RX0Carry USB serial and uploads. Wiring here breaks both — and they sit only four pins from TX2/RX2, so the mistake is easy.
VP · VN · D34 · D35Input only. They compile fine as outputs and silently do nothing.
D12Strapping pin — must read LOW at boot. A pull-up here stops the board starting.
D15 · D2 · D0Strapping / bootloader pins. D0 in particular blocks upload mode.
None of the pins in this build are used as analog inputs, so the usual "ADC2 stops working when Wi-Fi is on" problem does not apply here. If an analog sensor is ever added it must go on ADC1: GPIO 32, 33, 34, 35, 36 or 39.

Software built so far

Firmware — ItemScan.ino

The main terminal firmware. One scan cycle takes about 1.3 seconds.

WAIT     poll Clear every 80 ms; a drop past 60% of the empty-plate
         baseline means an item arrived
SETTLE   300 ms for it to come to rest
SENSE    average 5 colour reads
DECIDE   r/c ≥ 0.55 AND b/c ≥ 0.22  →  COPPER
POST     send to backend as known_material, ?compact=true
ACT      light the lamp
CLEAR    wait for the plate to empty, re-arm

The baseline is measured at boot, so the trigger adapts to whatever the room lighting is. Keep the plate empty while the board starts.

Ratios, not raw values — raw readings drifted tenfold across one day as the light changed, while the ratios held to within 0.01.

Cardboard and yellow sit 0.008 apart on r/c. Two colours obviously different to the eye are nearly identical to this sensor. The honest conclusion: copper versus not-copper is answerable; telling steel from nickel from zinc almost certainly is not, since all four are grey. That is consistent with the architecture — report §5 gives waste-type identification to Component 3's AI, and the design PDF casts this sensor as a confirmation aid that "verifies reddish Copper".

Open issues

  • Weight is a hardcoded 2.5 kg placeholder. No load cell here — it belongs to Component 3 and must arrive via the backend.
  • IR module overheated and is out of the build. Not blocking.
  • Grey metals may not separate by colour — needs confirming with real samples.
  • Bin-full lockout is not enforced in the backend, and nothing can clear one.
  • Wi-Fi credentials sit in the sketch and travel with the folder if it is shared.
  • The local LED rule contradicts the live forecast — copper lights yellow while the dashboard says SELL. Flip USE_BACKEND_DECISION to align them.
